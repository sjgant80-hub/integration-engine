The integration engine: a claim, built so it could fail

structure × self-reference · measured from outside ·

The hypothesis: deepening needs both new structure and self-reference, multiplied — because all structure and no self-reference is a blind pile, all self-reference and no structure is a stuck loop, and only the product climbs.

It is a good hypothesis. This page tests it, and the answer is not the one it predicted.

First: the original engine did not test it

The engine that proposed this set the self-reference term to a constant at every rung, so integration = structure × 1. Multiplying by a constant is not a merge. All eight rungs reported the product climbing while one of its two factors never moved:

So: make self-reference able to fail

Here a fold has a fixed number of parts, and parts spent holding a model of itself are parts not holding anything else. That is the real tension and it is not a metaphor — a system that watches itself is using capacity it could have spent on the work.

Then the score comes from outside: damage the fold, let it repair what it can, and count how much is still correct. Measured against the original, never against its own opinion of its depth.

The fair fight

Three climbs. Flat folds on itself forever and never grows. Geometric takes every form and never looks at itself. Merged does both every rung, and is allowed to choose how much to spend.

How good the self-model is allowed to be decides everything

This was nearly a rigged experiment. The first self-model stored duplicates of specific parts, so one part protected exactly one part — and under that encoding self-reference is a bad deal until the world is destroying half of you. Judging the claim against the worst possible implementation of it would have been the easy, wrong answer.

The verdict

The strong claim is false. Across 152 folds with both axes varied independently, structure × selfref never once beat structure on its own at predicting how much of a fold survives. Structure alone scores ρ 0.88–0.94 at every damage rate; the product scores 0.48–0.71. And self-reference on its own is near-useless as a predictor (|ρ| under 0.25 everywhere) — knowing how much a fold watches itself tells you almost nothing about how much of it survives.
The intuition behind it survives, sharpened. Self-reference is not a free multiplier, it is a purchase — and the right amount to buy tracks how hostile the environment is. At no damage, buy none. At 20%, buy 12%. At 70%, buy 40% and nearly double what survives. That is a design rule with a number in it, which the metaphor was not.

What this is

A toy formalism that runs, is internally consistent, and was built so the hypothesis could lose — which it did, in its strong form. It is not evidence about minds, and the resemblance to integrated information theory is a rhyme, not an implementation. What is genuinely established here is narrower and more useful than what was claimed: growth and self-monitoring trade against each other under a fixed budget, and self-monitoring only pays when something is actually corrupting you.
