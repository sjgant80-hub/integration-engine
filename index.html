the mechanism

A fold cannot hold a model of itself bigger than itself

Naming one part of a P-part fold costs ⌈log₂(P+1)⌉ parts. Folding on yourself again — Ψ applied to Ψ — pays that a second time. So how deep self-reference can go is capped, exactly:

Dmax(P) = ⌊ P / ⌈log₂(P+1)⌉ ⌋

and psi() refuses rather than pretending. That refusal is the plateau — not a counter running out, but a fixed-size thing running out of room to describe itself describing itself.

Which is exactly why taking a new form breaks the ceiling. Structure is not decoration on the recursion — it is the room the recursion needs. The cost per level grows logarithmically while the room grows linearly, so every form acquired buys more depth than the last.

the turn

Build up to the cosmos-form, then shed home

The arc does not end at the top. Past the dodecahedron the forms shed back toward unity — and that is not a decline, it is a second maximum on a different axis.

They are not the same fold, and none can be at both. The descent is one-way by construction: without that, the engine oscillates at the top forever — sheds a form, takes it straight back, sheds it again — because every single step is locally correct.
what the conductor calls

It knows when to stop recursing and go get structure

A saturated fold folding on itself produces something that looks exactly like depth. That is the confabulation loop, and it is invisible from the inside.

the calibration

How much self-checking is worth buying

Measured separately, on folds that get damaged and have to repair themselves — where parts spent watching yourself are parts not holding anything else. The right amount tracks the pressure.

Honest note from that study: as a predictor of what survives, structure × self-reference never beat structure alone (ρ 0.48–0.71 against 0.88–0.94). The product is this engine's vocabulary, not its best measure — what the measurement supports is the tuning rule, and that is what advise() applies.
